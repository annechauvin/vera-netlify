Your As-Is Analysis
What this is
As-Is Analysis takes the property exactly as it is today — the listing's current rents and expenses, no renovation assumed — and checks it against the following targets. The result is one of three verdicts: Turnkey (already clears every target as-is), BRRRR Potential (falls short as-is, but has a clear, fixable path — like undervalued rent or owner-paid utilities that could shift to tenants), or No-Go (falls short with no clear fix).
CriteriaTurnkeyBRRRR PotentialNo-Go
Cap rate5-6%+3-5%Below 3%
Cash flow / unit$200+/moBelow $200, may be slightly negativeDeeply negative
Total ROI20%+10-20%Below 10%
DSCR1.20+0-1.20Below 0
Gross rentBreak-even+May be below break-evenDeeply below break-even
1% rule1.00%+0.75%+Below 0.75%
These are guidelines, not hard rules — you're always free to move forward however you see fit. They're here to give you guardrails that keep your investment as safe as possible. Turnkey needs all three of cap rate, cash flow/unit, and total ROI. BRRRR Potential is expected to fall in the middle band — that's the point of the renovation. No-Go: cap rate below 3%, cash flow negative with no fixable cause, total ROI below 10%, DSCR below 0, or 1% rule below 0.75%.

Your Refinance Projection

          
        


        

          
What this is

          
The point of BRRRR is, after renovating, to refinance at the new, higher after-repair value (ARV) and pull cash back out. Enter the ARV estimate below — the refinance projection estimates how much cash comes back and is checked against the following targets. The result is one of three verdicts: Perfect BRRRR (you recover 100%+ of your initial investment — for seasoned investors), Good BRRRR (you recover 80%+ of your initial investment — for experienced investors), or Beginner BRRRR (you recover 60%+ of your initial investment — a solid deal for beginner BRRRR investors). If less than 60% of your initial investment is returned, it's essential to pause and evaluate your options: adjust your renovation plans, negotiate a lower offer price, or move on to another property.
